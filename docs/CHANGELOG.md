# Changelog

Newest first. One line per change that someone using or developing the office would notice.

## 2026-11-11
- **People, remodelled.** Employees, the foreman, other people in the office and the owner's own character are the same procedural figure as before, sculpted anew. **Face:** one surface with the nose, the lips and the hollows of the eyes in it; eyes with a white, an iris (six natural colours), a pupil and a catch-light; an upper lid with lashes that really closes (a blink, sleep, a wince) and a lower lid; brows with a shape; ears with a rim. **Hair:** every style rebuilt with a clean hairline that ends in the skin — short (clipped sides, combed-over top with a parting), long (to the shoulders, one surface inside and out), bun, curly (one rounded head of curls instead of two hundred balls), each also under a cap, with headphones and with glasses. **Body and clothes:** a chest, a waist and sloping shoulders the sleeve comes out from under; a larger head (×1.14) for a figure that reads by its face; the polo (soft collar, placket, two buttons, belt), the shirt (crisp collar, buttons down the front, cuffs, belt), the T-shirt (ribbed neck, loose hem), the sweater (knitted, rolled neck, ribbed hem and cuffs) and the suit are five different garments in five different cloths; skin is matt; hands have grouped fingers and a thumb; leather shoes with a welt, and trainers with a white sole for the T-shirt and the sweater.
- **No two people quite alike, with no new settings:** the face (three), the brows (three), the colour of the eyes, the side the hair is parted on, the width of the head and how broad the trunk is all follow from the look a person already has. The same look always gives the same person; the outfit, tie and shoes are chosen as before.
- Cost: a person is 19–26 thousand triangles (was 16–28), one draw call fewer close up (24 for short hair and a polo, was 25), and every part now shares its corners between triangles; measured figures are in STATUS.
- For developers: the character code is split into `packages/office-3d/src/character/` (`motion.ts`, `shape.ts`, `head.ts`, `body.ts`, `look.ts`) with `Character.tsx` as the component; `packages/office-3d/test/character.test.ts` (sound surfaces, a triangle budget, the same look gives the same person); `scripts/character-sheet.cjs` captures every hair style × accessory, every outfit on three skin tones, close-ups, poses, the same people small, and counts flicker; `window.__orchScene.lineup = [{ appearance, pose, level, x, z, rot, still? }]` stands figures on the floor for a capture.

## 2026-11-10
- **The office's furniture, floors and fittings, remodelled.** Every box that stood for a thing is now that thing, sculpted in code (no asset files): task chairs with a five-star base, castors, gas lift and a back that wraps round the sitter; desks with an oak top on a steel frame, a drawer pedestal, a monitor on a stand, a keyboard with keys and a mouse; cloth dividers; three kinds of potted plant; sofas and armchairs with loose cushions; a slatted reception counter; a walnut meeting table on trestles with visitor chairs; the owner's desk with an executive chair and a rug; a kitchen run with sink, tap, coffee machine, cups and fruit, and a fridge; racks with the machines visible behind their doors; the operators' console, the day's numbers on its sloped deck; bookshelves of books; a floor lamp; the hi-fi (walnut sideboard, receiver, turntable, speakers with real cones); the media cabinet under the TV; clocks with a bezel; boards in rounded frames with a marker tray. Occupied desks carry a cup, a notebook and sometimes a small plant (which ones follows from the desk, so they do not change).
- **Floors and building.** Carpet tiles on the engineering floor, oak boards in the owner's office, stone in the hall and at reception, ceramic tiles in the break room, a raised floor under the racks and consoles; a tiled suspended ceiling; a skirting board along every wall; posts between the panes of glass walls.
- **Earned decorations now appear.** The arcade cabinet, the trophy case and the neon sign were never shown: the office asked for them by the milestone's key (`ten_tasks`) while the app offered what the milestone grants (`arcade`). One function (`unlockedUpgrades`, contracts) now gives both names. All three are modelled (the arcade's screen and marquee, cups in a lit glass case, neon lettering with the product's tagline). The earned **team room** is furnished (a table for six, a sofa) unless the owner has put desks or decorations there; its floor no longer flickers against the engineering floor it lies on.
- Characters: a fleck of light in each eye (it closes with a blink); curly hair is rounder up close.
- For developers: `packages/office-3d/src/models/` (the kit and the models), `packages/office-3d/test/models.test.ts` (every model fits its floor area, keeps seat and desk heights, stays within a triangle budget), `window.__orchScene.cam = { pos, look, fov? }` to hold the camera for a capture, `window.__orchScene.positions` (where everyone is).
- **Movement and animation, reworked.** Faster and lighter: the everyday pace is a jog (3.8 u/s forward, was 2.5), Shift is a run (7.4), turning 2.9 rad/s, up to speed in 0.13 s and stopped in 0.12 s; the camera follows more closely. The gait is chosen from speed and direction (stride, cadence, flight phase at a run), with heel strike and toe-off, hip drop, counter-rotating shoulders, arm swing, lean into speed and turns, a settled idle with weight shifts, and feet that land where the body will stop. Feet no longer slide: slip is at or under 1.2% of distance in every direction (was 7–13%, and up to 79% for employees at uneven frame times). Employees walk at 1.9 u/s, ease in and out of a walk and turn to face their chair before sitting. Known weak spots: the strafe at full speed is a crossover side-jog rather than a pure sidestep, the backpedal is a low shuffle, employees still pass through their chair when sitting down.
- **The foreman, reworked.** There is one for the whole office, it runs on the office's own machine and it has **no desk**: its character walks the office — the shared floor and members' rooms that are open — stands with whoever it is dealing with during a round, and waits by the entrance while rounds are paused. Team shows it on a line of its own; the hire form has no desk step for it; an existing foreman gives up its desk when the office starts.
- The foreman now looks after **everyone, members' own employees included** (while their computer is connected): it sees their terminals, answers their questions and tells them what to do. Work still never crosses between the shared office and a member's computer. Because of what it sees, the foreman itself is **for owners only**: its terminal, documents and history, talking to it, its profile, and hiring one.
- New attention kind **idle**: a running session that has done nothing for 10 minutes (Settings → Foreman, 0 = never) with no task in progress is put in front of the foreman once per spell; if only the foreman has spoken to them since, they are left alone twice as long each time. New foreman tool **`tell_employee`** (carry on, how a blocker is resolved, what to pick up). `GET /api/foreman` says who it is with (`visiting`); `foreman.visit` events now also record answers, assignments and instructions.
- Hiring a foreman switches its rounds on (Settings → Foreman pauses them). A foreman's folder is no longer turned into a project, and a refused foreman hire leaves nothing behind.
- **Knocking at the door.** Someone without an invitation can ask to be let in: in a browser, the office's address (`https://office.sparkcan.com`) now shows the office's name and a place to knock; in the desktop app, typing the address instead of pasting an invitation does the same. Owners and colleagues who are inside hear a knock and see a card (name, how they came, a four-digit code the knocker sees too). An owner lets an app user in as visitor, colleague or owner; a colleague can let someone in to look; **a browser can only ever be let in as a visitor**, and that visit ends by itself after 12 hours. The app keeps what it was given as an ordinary invitation, so next time it walks straight in. Nothing is granted by knocking itself (`apps/server/src/knocks.ts`; limits per address; `tests/e2e/15-knock.test.ts`).
- **"They answered you".** When you say something to an employee (Talk) and walk away, three dots hang over their head while they work on it; when their turn ends, a soft chime, a card at the top right ("Ava answered you" with the first lines) and a bubble over their head stay until you open the conversation. Only your own conversations: what employees say to each other, or what other people ask, puts nothing there. Kept by this computer across a reload.
- **What is said on a member's own computer stays theirs.** Feed lines (which carry an employee's replies), messages and questions about a member's personal employees are now sent only to that member and to owners — in the state, in `GET /api/events` and on the live connection. Before, other colleagues and visitors received them (they could not open the terminal, but the feed told them what it said).
- Chat box at the bottom left, as in an online game: what people said and did (`[19:37] [Name] says: …`, emotes in orange), lit for 20 s after a new line and on hover, with the last hour told to whoever walks in (`chat.history`). Enter always opens the line. What you say is also a bubble over **your own** character (not in first person).
- A crack of the whip is seen by everyone in the office: the person's character swings, the employee flinches, and it is in the chat (`whip` → `whipped`; one crack per 600 ms per person). Still only a gag: nothing reaches the employee's session.
- Hiring is three steps: **where** (the shared office on the server, or my own room on my computer — asked, not assumed), **the folder** on that machine (the system folder dialog only when the machine is the one the window is on; otherwise the machine's own folder list), **the desk** (any free desk in the office, or a seat in my room; "Automatic" stays). Fixes hiring from a client into the server office, which offered the client's folder dialog for the server's disk.
- Seats in a member's room can be chosen and are kept (`deskId` `m:<host>:<n>`); the room grows to the furthest chosen seat.
- A refused change to an employee no longer leaves them half-changed in memory until the next restart.
- Usage clocks: the CEO room shows the Claude accounts of the office's own (shared) machines; each member's own account hangs as a clock on a wall of their room. Everyone may read the percentages (`GET /api/usage` is now a *read*); the account's e-mail and organisation are sent only to those who may work on that machine.
- `office.sparkcan.com` is live (Let's Encrypt certificate on the server's nginx). The office window's WebSocket now presents its token as a subprotocol instead of in the address; the proxy does not log office requests.
- `office.sparkcan.com`: CloudPanel reverse-proxy site in front of the office's door; the desktop app now also accepts an office whose certificate is publicly trusted for its name (pinning stays for addresses with the office's own certificate).
- `pnpm deploy:server` (`scripts/deploy-server.sh`: build, copy, swap, restart, `--status`, `--rollback`) and `docs/OPERATIONS.md` (where everything runs, how to update server and clients, backup, troubleshooting).
- Members arrange their own room (Menu → My room → *Arrange my room*): name on the door, colour, number of desks (up to 20), open floor behind the desks, furniture. Kept by the office (`PUT /api/me/room`), drawn for everyone.
- A member's room now stays in the office when they leave: grey, dark and locked, their employees asleep at their desks; it opens again when their computer reconnects. Rooms no longer change places when someone leaves.
- A saved office can be marked "open at start": the app walks into it without the start screen. The owner's local office was shut down and set aside (see STATUS); they now work in the server office.
