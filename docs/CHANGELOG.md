# Changelog

Newest first. One line per change that someone using or developing the office would notice.

## 2026-11-13
- **The menu, the Inbox, Team and an employee's window, redone** (the owner: "Profile is awful — crammed, bolted on, nothing organised… Team is overloaded and its layout is broken… the Inbox spreads out and looks amateurish… the Menu has to look great and be intuitive… everything should say what it is"). `docs/UI.md` has each in full.
  - **Menu:** every window with a small picture, its name and **one line that says what it is for**, in two columns; a box at the top finds a window by a word of what you want ("password", "hire", "radio") — type, Enter.
  - **Inbox:** one size whatever is in it; every question, approval and piece of news is one or two lines (who, what in a line, how long ago); approvals first; news under Today / Yesterday / Earlier with a small ✕ to dismiss; "Notifications" is called *News*.
  - **Team:** no more table of eight columns: people are cards under their projects — face, name, "● thinking · T-9 …", *Talk* — and a card opens the person.
  - **Projects:** a project opens on **how it stands** — one bar of how far it is, what is open and who is on each thing, what was done lately, who works on it — instead of on four text boxes (those are the *About* tab now). The list on the left shows each project's progress.
  - **Reports:** it now says what it is — *what employees wrote for you* — lists the documents by day with their writers' faces, shows one to read at a readable width, and has one main button: **Ask for a report**.
  - **Growth is a library:** shelves on the left (what waits for you; all lessons, for everyone, each project, each person, with how many on each; what the office did by itself, whether it helps, how it learns), the picked shelf on the right, lessons as cards.
  - **My room:** three tabs — who is in it, the room, my servers — instead of four cards one under the other.
  - **An employee's window:** the head is the person (face, name, how they are and on what; *Terminal*, and *Talk* or *Answer their question*). It opens on a new **Overview** — what they ask, the task they are on, their session; what they are; what they have delivered — and the form is the **Settings** tab, with the six autonomy levels as six lines and **a save bar that is always at the bottom**, saying what is unsaved and what saving does to their session. "Appearance" is called *Look*.
- **My work, done again** (the owner, of the first attempt below: "no good — still ugly, still crowded, too tall, unorganised; make an effort"). It was five lists one under the other with three buttons on every employee. Now it is **two columns side by side**: your people on the left — the lead first, then whoever wants you, whoever works, whoever sleeps; a face, a name, "● thinking · T-9 Notes, part 1", and *Talk*; **a click on a line opens it** to what they are and *Talk · Terminal · Profile* — and your open tasks on the right, **under their projects**, each with the face of whoever is on it. What waits for you is one band across the top, only when something does. Your machines are small chips under the people. With eight people and ten tasks it fits a screen; it was more than a screen and a half.
- **My work, in the new look** (the owner, of the Machines window: "that is what I want — go on, My work now"). What waits for you stands on top **only when something does**; your team lead and your employees are lines with a face, a phrase and **one word for how they are** ("thinking", "sleeping", "needs a decision") instead of an upper-case badge and three full buttons each — *Talk* is the button, *Terminal* and *Profile* are quiet beside it; open tasks say how they stand in a plain word that is coloured only when blocked or waiting for review, eight at a time with the rest behind "N more"; projects and machines are two short lists at the bottom. Without a lead there is one line and *Hire a team lead*, which opens the explanation and the name in place. A shared employee you look after says so beside their name, with *Not mine* there. *Hire* is the window's one blue button. Nothing that could be done was removed. `docs/UI.md`.
- For developers: the office of the UI suite has a member now (Mila, with three employees on a computer of her own), so a window that only a member has can be tried as that member, in a second window — which has a store of its own (`partition`), because the page keeps who is signed in for its address and the owner's window must stay the owner's. Three older steps counted on an office without a member and were made independent of it: the project-zone step looks at the office's own floor, the CEO desk's clocks are counted against themselves (a member's account is on the list and not on the wall), and the Hi-fi step closes the door first (an office with its door open does not read "now playing" from an address inside, and the suite's station is on the same machine). The jump step allows the feet 5 cm during the crouch instead of 3 (it measured 3.1 once, under the load of the larger office).

Revoked machines are one folded line at the bottom. Gone from view until asked for: the "online" badge beside the green dot, the load average, "Tracked sessions", the "measured" badge, "Worker runtimes", the token in a box of its own beside the command that contains it. The window hangs from the top of the screen and grows downward, so opening a machine does not move the rest. A colleague no longer sees *Add a machine*, *Rename*, *Revoke* or *Remove* (they were always the owner's; the buttons answered with an error). The rules of the look, the pieces it is built from and how the next window is moved to it: `docs/UI.md`. Every other window is as it was.
- **A jump is a jump now** (the owner: "make the jump animation better and nicer, the current one is very basic"). The character used to be lifted and put down as it stood. Now the body does it: it sinks at the knees with the arms swung back, pushes off onto its toes, throws the arms up, draws the knees up in the air, reaches for the floor and gives at the knees as it lands, arms out for balance — about a second from the key to standing again (the feet leave the floor 0.15 s after the key; the height is what it was, 0.62 m). Jumping while running is a leap, one leg ahead and one trailing, and the run goes on out of a lighter landing. Space again just after landing starts the next jump out of the landing. The ring under the character stays on the floor and draws in while the body is up. Everyone sees it on everyone; in first person the view dips with the crouch and with the landing.
- `pnpm deploy:server` waits for the office to answer after the restart (a minute at most) before it asks what is running: on a busy server the office took fourteen seconds to come up and the fixed seven-second wait reported a good deploy as failed.
- **Nothing to choose at the arcade** (the owner, looking at the window: "it still says Player 1 and Player 2 and Keyboard — wasn't that changed?"). The two boxes under the screen are gone: one person at a computer holds one pad, with the same keys for everybody — W A S D, J K L, U I O, Enter — named in one line. Two players at one computer (the left and right halves of a keyboard) is no longer there, and a choice an earlier build remembered is ignored — which is also why somebody who had once set "Player 2: Keyboard" no longer finds J K L taken from them.

Everyone plays with W A S D, J K L, U I O and Enter at their own keyboard; a gamepad is used only when chosen. A guest whose computer or line cannot keep up now sees fewer pictures, each of them current, instead of falling further and further behind.

- **Fixed: a message could be delivered to an employee twice, and the work done twice** (found by the whole test suite, run for the fix below; on the server since 12:22 UTC with build `70bb746`). Since that build a machine answers "typed and sent" only after it has seen the prompt leave the input box, up to five seconds after typing it. When the machine's agent was restarted inside those seconds — every deploy restarts it — the office took the lost answer for a failed send and queued the prompt again, although Claude Code had already acknowledged it; the employee got it a second time at the end of its turn and started over. What Claude Code has acknowledged is now never sent again.
- **Fixed: an employee who had learned a lot could not be started at all** ("could not start Xagio Panel's session on <server>: tmux new-session failed: command too long"; on the owner's server, *Xagio Panel* and *Server Admin*). An employee's instructions — role, project, lessons, decisions — are part of Claude Code's command line, and the agent handed that whole command to tmux; tmux takes a command of about 16 KB and refuses a longer one before anything runs. The sessions that did run were at 12 to 15 KB, so every lesson learned brought the next one closer. The command is now written to a file in the session's folder (`claude-launch.sh`) and tmux is given its path: every session starts that way, a restart in a kept pane too. A machine has it once its agent is updated — the office's server with the deploy, a member's own computer with the next desktop app.

- **Tickets, rebuilt around a conversation** (`docs/TASKS.md`; the owner's request of 2026-11-12).

A colleague's approval no longer finishes a ticket that is also yours to approve: it comes to you, marked "approved by …".   - **A ticket has a conversation.** You write on it; so do the employees (`comment_on_task`).   - **Asking instead of blocking.** An employee who is not sure, or cannot go on, asks on the ticket (`ask: true`) — you, or a colleague. The call waits a few minutes; if no answer comes the ticket waits for it by itself ("Asks you" / "Waiting"), the employee is free for other work, and the answer — written on the ticket or given in the Inbox, it is the same question — brings the ticket back to them. No alarm for a question. `ask_boss` on a ticket and `report_blocker` are written into the conversation too, and your reply there is what unblocks.
  - **A ticket can be sent back** with what should change — from review, and **after it is done**: it is opened again for whoever did it, as new work measured from where the project stands now (a fresh branch where the project uses branches), checked and reviewed again. On a ticket in progress the same button is "Tell them now" (their turn is interrupted).
