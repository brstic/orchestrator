## What exists (verified by the automated suites)
- **Office on a server**: `pnpm serve` (server + shared host agent + TLS door), `pnpm office …` to invite and manage members from the server's terminal, a systemd unit generator.
- **Members and roles**: visitor (reads shared), colleague (works on shared, own computer), owner (everything). One access module (`apps/server/src/access.ts`); see `ACCESS.md`.
- **Hiring asks where**: shared office or my room, then the folder on that machine, then the desk or seat (Team → Hire; both the single form and "one per project folder").
- **Rooms are permanent and arrangeable**: each member's room stays in the office when they are away (grey, locked, employees asleep) and the member sets its name, colour, desks, open floor and furniture from Menu → My room.
- **Personal hosts and member rooms**: the desktop app of a colleague/owner enrolls their computer through the door (pinned relay + host agent); their employees run there and sit in a room attached to the office while they are connected. "My room" panel to add folders and adopt local Claude sessions.
- **Per-client preferences**: graphics, keys, office extras per computer; everyone can change their own.
- Local office (one machine) as before: 3D office, real Claude Code sessions in tmux, tasks, reviews/merges, meetings, questions and approvals, self-improvement, XP.
- Other shared machines: enrolled hosts, servers linked over SSH, adoption of running Claude sessions.
- Claude usage per account: the office's accounts as wall clocks in the CEO room and at the CEO desk, each member's own account as a clock in their room.
- **The foreman**: one for the whole office, on the office's own machine, with no desk — it walks the floor and members' open rooms and stands with whoever it is dealing with. It supervises shared employees and members' own (while their computer is connected), in engine-driven rounds: questions, blocked, stalled, unassigned and idle; tools to look, answer, assign, create, tell, escalate. Owners-only. Rounds are on once it is hired.
- **Knocking**: with only the office's address, a browser (read-only visit, 12 h) or the desktop app asks to come in; owners and colleagues inside answer the door (`ACCESS.md`, "The door bell").
- **Answer cue**: dots over an employee you spoke to, then a card and a bubble when they have answered (your own conversations only, per computer).
- People in the office: characters with name tags, speech bubbles (also over one's own head), a chat box at the bottom left with the last hour of history, and the whip seen by everyone.
- **A modelled office**: furniture, floors, ceiling and fittings sculpted in code (`packages/office-3d/src/models/`; ARCHITECTURE.md, Layer C) — chairs, desks with what stands on them, plants, sofas, reception, meeting table, the owner's desk, kitchen, racks, console, shelves, hi-fi. The arcade cabinet, trophy case, neon sign and the furnished team room appear when the company has earned them.
- **Remodelled people** (CHANGELOG 2026-11-11): a sculpted face with real eyes and lids, hair per style, five distinct garments, small differences from person to person derived from their look.
- Extras: radio, TV, whip, seating editor, sleeping pose. Storage: SQLite (default) and PostgreSQL.

## Not verified / known problems
- **The remodelled people (CHANGELOG 2026-11-11) have not been seen by the owner, and are not deployed.** They were looked at only in captures of a development office on the development MacBook, High quality: a sheet of every hair style × accessory, every outfit on three skin tones, close-ups, the seated and standing poses, the same people at the overhead camera's size (`scripts/character-sheet.cjs`), and in the office itself — at desks, asleep on a desk, the raised hand, the owner's character from behind, the side and the front, jogging, the whip. Not looked at: Medium and Low quality (no antialiasing on Low), a 60 Hz display, Windows or Linux graphics, first-person mode, the foreman walking, other people's characters (`Humans.tsx`), a talking pose in a meeting.
  - **Measured there** in a 1500×940 window with the frame rate uncapped, median of eight two-second samples, new against old: from reception with 13 people 2.44 ms a frame against 2.45 (709 draw calls both, 789 000 triangles against 781 000); among the desks 2.32 against 2.38 (546 calls against 530, 715 000 triangles against 688 000); with 40 more people standing in view at full detail 5.6 against 5.1–5.5; with 120 more 11.0 against 11.1–12.4. At the display's 144 Hz both hold 144 frames a second with 40 more people (worst frame 8.7 ms) and both fall to 72 with 120. One person close up: 23–29 draw calls (was 23–30) and 19 500–25 800 triangles (was 15 900–27 600; short hair and a polo 21 700, was 21 100).
  - **Flicker**, counted on composited frames (pixels that change and change back) with ten figures in view against the build before: close up 0–1 pixels a frame (before 62), at five metres 8–64 (before 33), from the overhead camera and from across the building the count is dominated by something that is not the people — the same view with nobody in it gives 2–480 from one run to the next, in both builds (the unexplained single-frame changes noted below). Nothing in the new figure lies on another surface at less than a millimetre except the lids on the eye (1.1 mm, close-up parts that are left out beyond 30 m). **Not judged by eye on a real display.**
  - **What still does not look right:** the nose is soft seen exactly from the front (it reads from any other angle); curly hair is a tousled mass more than separate curls; long hair is one smooth bob, rigid, and passes through a shoulder when the head turns far; the cap's peak meets the crown with a visible step; the shirt collar under a jacket is a little ragged at its corners at 0.6 m; trousers are plain tubes without a crease and the seat is flat from the side; the hand of the thinking pose still stops short of the chin (as before); mouths do not move when talking and faces have one expression. The 2D portrait in profiles (`Avatar`) is unchanged: skin, hair and shirt colours only.
- **The remodelled office (CHANGELOG 2026-11-10) has not been seen by the owner.** It was looked at in captures of a development office — every room, overhead and at eye level, High quality, on the development MacBook — and measured there (below). Not looked at: Medium and Low quality, a 60 Hz display, Windows or Linux graphics, an office with many member rooms. Deployed to the server office on 2026-11-10 (clients get it with a reload); the UI bundled inside `release/Orchestrator.app` is older and is only used for a local office.
- Flicker of the new models, measured on composited frames with people and overlays hidden, against the build before: standing still it is the same (0–6 pixels a frame change and change back). Walking with the overhead camera about 90 pixels a frame against 40. Walking at eye level about 1 200 against 11: almost all of it is the lines of the new ceiling grid and floor joints passing by, which this count cannot tell from flicker (the one thing that did crawl, the reception counter's battens at 15 000, was changed). **Not judged by eye on a real display.** In both builds, with people in view, a few hundred pixels sometimes change for one frame while standing still; the cause was not found.
- The building itself is still bare: no doors in the doorways, no windows, nothing outside. People still pass through their chair when they sit down or get up.
- Knocking: tried with the real desktop app and a plain browser window against a test office; **not yet against `office.sparkcan.com` by a person on another network**. The app knocks only where the certificate is one the computer trusts (the domain); an office reached by IP address still needs an invitation.
